The painting is not meant to be settled at first glance. Its pink field
gives a quick first impression, but that impression is only the cover. Like
a story that asks the reader to turn the page before judging it, the work
opens up through time spent with it.

Lines run across the surface and connect one cluster of marks to the next,
so the eye moves from episode to episode instead of taking in a single
image. Some passages are dense and crowded. Others thin out into loose
drawing near the bottom, as if the story is fading into memory. The first
look is only where the reading begins.
